A Moment of Stillness for Thought 



It has been raining a lot lately in the chilly city of Jos, and it continued to shower this morning. I made a strong cup of coffee and took a seat to focus on my own development. As I was looking through Spotify for the ideal background track to code to, I realized the following: *Why is it that I find solitude to be so appealing? Does someone who aspires to program benefit from or suffer from having an introverted personality?*

I love the art of programming because it requires deep thinking, twisted logic, and the thrill of creating something from scratch. However, remember the satisfaction of finally debugging an error after hours of frustration. But, to be honest, it has been a solitary journey filled with hours spent in deep thought, often lost in my own world.

And here's the thing: I can't blame programming for this isolation. The reality is, my introversion has likely played a big role. You know, it's easy to lost yourself in the world of logic and algorithm where communication takes place internally; foressing problems, attempting fixes, learning new things, and repeating. similar to a loop. I became more adept at solving issues on my own and less dependant on assistance from others. whereas I might be becoming disoriented in the actual world.

I have come to realize that the solitude, I sometimes find myself, isn't just the by-product of my career choice but a reflection of who I am.

Yet I find myself questioning: *Is this really where I belong?*

Programming requires deep focus, critical thinking and a lot of alone time. For someone like me, who thrives in solitude, it's a perfect fit. But it also requires collaboration, communication, and the ability to network and connect with others to create something greater than what one person can achieve alone. Is it possible that my introversion has made me overlook the value of connection?

As I sit here, sipping my coffee and watching the world outside slowly come back to life after the rain. I realize that this path I've chosen is more than just a career. It is a mirror reflecting my personality, my strength and challenges. And maybe, just maybe, it's a reminder that while programming might be a solitary journey at times, it doesn't have to be a lonely one. I'm determined to find ways to balance my "lone wolf" nature with the collaborative aspects of programming.

The rain had just finished its nonstop downpour, leaving the city of jos in a cool, chill state. I brewed myself a hot cup of coffee and settled in for a coding session. As I scrolled through Spotify, searching for that perfect background music to code to,a thought struck me: *Why am I so drawn to solitude? Is this introversion a natural fit for programming, or is it a flaw?*

I love the art of programming: the deep thinking, the logic twists, the thrills of solving a complex problem. I remember the satisfaction of finally debugging an error after hours of frustration, But, to be honest, it has been a solitary journey... a journey filled with hours spent in deep thought, often lost in my own world.

And here's the thing: I can't blame programming for this isolation. The reality is, my introversion has likely played a big role. You know, it's easy to engulf yourself in the world of logic and algorithm where conversations are internal; foressing problems, trying out solutions, learning new things, fixing things, and repeating. Just like a loop... I learned to fix problems on my own quicker without needing the help of someone else's help. 

I have come to realize that the solitude, I sometimes find myself, isn't just the by-product of my career choice but a reflection of who I am.

Yet I find myself questioning: *Is this really where I belong?*

Programming requires deep focus, critical thinking and a lot of alone time. For someone like me, who thrives in solitude, it's a perfect fit. But it also requires collaboration, communication, and the ability to network and connect with others to create something greater than what one person can achieve alone. Is it possible that my introversion has made me overlook the value of connection?

As I sit here, sipping my coffee and watching the world outside slowly come back to life after the rain. I realize that this path I've chosen is more than just a career. It is a mirror reflecting my personality, my strength and challenges. And maybe, just maybe, it's a reminder that while programming might be a solitary journey at times, it doesn't have to be a lonely one. I'm determined to find ways to balance my "lone wolf" nature with the collaborative aspects of programming.
